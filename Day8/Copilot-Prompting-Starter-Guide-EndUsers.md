# Copilot Prompting Starter Guide — For FinBridge Office Staff

**Audience:** Everyone using Microsoft 365 Copilot (Outlook, Word, Excel) — no technical background needed
**Prepared by:** DWP Engineering

## What this guide is for

Copilot is only as useful as the prompt you give it. This guide gives you a simple formula for writing good prompts, plus real examples for Outlook, Word, and Excel that you can copy, adapt, and reuse today.

No jargon, no theory — just prompts you can actually use.

---

## The good prompt formula

Struggling to think of what to type? Use this simple formula every time:

> **Task + Context + Format = Good Prompt**

- **Task** — what do you actually want Copilot to do? (summarise, draft, rewrite, calculate, explain)
- **Context** — what information does it need to do it well? (the email thread, the document, the data, the audience)
- **Format** — how do you want the answer to look? (a bullet list, a table, 3 sentences, a formal tone, a formula)

**Example:**
> "Summarise this email thread (**task**) about the Q3 budget delay (**context**) in 3 bullet points for my manager (**format**)."

If your prompt feels vague, check it against the formula — you're usually missing the **context** or the **format**.

A good habit: if Copilot's first answer isn't quite right, don't start over — just reply with what to change, e.g. *"Make it shorter"* or *"More formal, please."* Copilot remembers the conversation.

## Outlook prompts

Open the email or thread you want help with, then look for the Copilot icon (a small sparkle) near the top of the message. Click it and a box will appear where you can just type in plain English — no special commands needed.

1. **Summarise a long thread**
   > "Summarise this email thread in 5 bullet points, highlighting any decisions made and anything still waiting on me."

2. **Draft a reply**
   > "Draft a polite reply letting the sender know the report will be delayed until Friday due to a data issue, and ask if that's still workable for their deadline."

3. **Prepare before a meeting**
   > "Look at my emails from this week with Sarah Jenkins and summarise the open questions I still need to answer before our meeting tomorrow."

---

## Word prompts

Open your document, then click the Copilot icon (a small sparkle, usually top-right). A box opens where you can type what you need — you can also ask it to write a brand-new document from nothing.

1. **Draft a first version from bullet points**
   > "Turn these bullet points into a first draft of a one-page project update for senior management: [paste your bullet points]."

2. **Rewrite tone or length**
   > "Rewrite this paragraph in a more formal tone, and shorten it to 4 sentences."

3. **Summarise a long document**
   > "Summarise this 10-page policy document into a half-page overview for someone who hasn't read it, using plain English."

---

## Excel prompts

Open your workbook, then click the Copilot icon (a small sparkle, usually top-right). A box opens where you can type what you need — Copilot works best when your data is in a normal table with column headings.

1. **Explain the data**
   > "Look at this spreadsheet and give me a 3-bullet summary of the key trends in the Sales column over the last 6 months."

2. **Build a formula**
   > "Write a formula that calculates the total spend per department from the Amount and Department columns, and show it as a table."

3. **Highlight and flag**
   > "Highlight any rows where the Budget Variance is over 10%, and add a column that flags them as 'Review Needed'."

## Data-safety reminder — this still applies to Copilot

**The good news first:** Microsoft 365 Copilot — the one built into Outlook, Word, and Excel, and the only tool covered in this guide — runs safely inside FinBridge's own protected Microsoft 365 environment. It only ever looks at things you're already allowed to see, and it never sends your prompts out to the public internet or to outside AI companies. For everyday use of the prompts above, you don't need to worry.

There's just one habit worth carrying over from Day 1: the [DWP Personal AI Usage Charter](../Day1/DWP-Personal-AI-Usage-Charter.md) set the rule that we never paste client data, personal details, or passwords into a **public or external** AI tool — think free chatbots you find on the open internet, or AI add-ons that sit outside FinBridge's managed systems. That's a different, unprotected category of tool, separate from the Microsoft 365 Copilot this guide is about:

- **Microsoft 365 Copilot (Outlook, Word, Excel)** — safe for everyday use, stays inside FinBridge's protected systems.
- **Any public or external AI tool** — keep client data, personal details, and passwords out of these, always.

Not sure if a tool counts as an approved FinBridge Microsoft 365 tool or an external one? Just ask your manager or IT — it's a quick, welcome question, not a mistake.
